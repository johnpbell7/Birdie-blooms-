About Birdie Blooms

Hello, I’m Hannah.

I’m the florist, the buyer, the delivery driver and the person who answers your emails. Birdie Blooms is a one-woman flower studio, and I wouldn’t have it any other way.

Read the story

The story

It started on a kitchen table.

Birdie Blooms began with a bucket of market flowers, a pair of borrowed scissors and a habit of giving bunches away to anyone who’d stand still long enough.

Friends started asking for birthday flowers, then a friend asked for her wedding flowers, and somewhere along the way the kitchen table stopped being big enough. Now there’s a proper studio in Harpenden, Hertfordshire, a wall of buckets and a delivery van that always smells faintly of sweet peas.

What hasn’t changed is how it works. Every order is still made by me, by hand, on the morning it leaves. I buy fresh each week, choose British-grown stems whenever they’re in season, and you’ll speak to me from your first message to the moment your flowers arrive.

I love flowers that look like they’ve wandered in from the garden: loose, a little wild, and full of whatever the season is doing. If that sounds like your kind of thing, I’d love to make something for you.

Hannah

How I work

Small studio, clear rules.

No vague "eco" claims. Here’s exactly what happens in the studio, and what doesn’t.

No floral foam, ever.

Foam is a single-use plastic that sheds microplastics. Every arrangement here is built with reusable chicken wire, moss, pin frogs and water tubes instead.

Paper, not plastic.

Recycled paper, cotton ribbon and string. Bouquets are tied and wrapped in water so they arrive drinking.

British-grown first.

Whenever the season allows, stems come from British growers. When it doesn’t, I say so.

Local, by hand.

Delivered by me within twelve miles of the studio, so flowers spend hours, not days, out of water.

A week in the studio

Early starts, wet sleeves.

  1. Monday

    Market and growers. An early start at the wholesale market, then a loop of local growers to see what’s ready. Whatever’s best comes home.

  2. Tuesday

    Conditioning day. Every stem is cleaned, cut and given a long drink in cool water before it goes anywhere near a bouquet.

  3. Wednesday to Friday

    Making and delivering. Bouquets are made each morning and delivered by me in the afternoon. Wedding planning happens in between.

  4. Saturday

    Weddings and the last deliveries of the week. Buttonholes pinned, arches built, vases collected.

  5. Sunday

    The garden, and a very large cup of tea.
